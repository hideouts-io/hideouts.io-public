What location data can actually tell us

A Mac can hold references to cities, radio networks and places its owner has never visited. Put those references on a map and the picture looks persuasive: thousands of precise points, carrier names and identifiers. The difficult question is what each point represents.

The Local Evidence Atlas makes that question visible. It separates map resources, network-position estimates, coverage summaries and infrastructure records, then keeps each conclusion attached to its evidence. Its most useful result is an exact connection between a public NR identity and two position estimates. The remaining gap is equally specific: neither a measured PCI nor an actual installation has been established for that identity.

Scope: Read-only research into retained macOS map resources and deliberately acquired public Downtown Los Angeles cellular data. The full-map research preview preserves the prepared technical catalog with only personal hardware and access information removed. The owner has authorized its research publication. How the investigation developed

The first lead was a saved boot-log excerpt mentioning locationd cache categories such as coarse-cdma, coarse-lte and coarse-nr. The selected entries showed directory enumeration, with only . and .. and a zero file count. That prompted a read-only examination of local location-service caches and map resources. The log did not establish a cellular connection or a serving-cell identity.

The investigation then developed in several stages. The dates below describe review and acquisition work in November 2026, not visits, radio observations or per-record measurement dates.

What this Atlas adds

The Atlas adds a specific preserved collection, native-parser cross-checks, explicit coordinate roles, exact-identity comparisons and a tested interface for exploring the evidence together. The Downtown LA comparison connects one full NR identity to two estimates; the coverage-parser trace explains why the coverage fields do not complete the identity-to-site chain.

This bounded comparison does not establish first publication of the exact coverage-schema trace, full camera-frame set or combined collection. A measured target PCI and an independently supported physical-site link also remain unresolved. The missing-link criteria describe the evidence needed to answer those questions. Prior work's findings retain their own experimental scope; their existence is not evidence of surveillance or remote control on this computer.

Read the identifiers before connecting the dots

MCC/MNC identifies a mobile network assignment. The Atlas labels 310/260 as T-Mobile, 310/410 as AT&T and 311/480 as Verizon, while preserving the original codes. These names help searching; they do not establish ownership of nearby equipment or a subscriber’s retail brand. Assignments and dates are retained separately. US IMSI Administrator assignments.

ECI is the full LTE cell identity; NCI is its NR counterpart. NCI has 36 bits, whereas LTE ECI has 28. A full NR identity is interpreted with its PLMN, rather than as an isolated number. TAC identifies a tracking area, not an antenna. ETSI TS 23.003, clauses 19.4.2.9.3, 19.6 and 19.6A.

PCI is a reusable physical-layer identity. NR has 1,008 possibilities. NRARFCN identifies the radio channel. A PCI plus a carrier and channel narrows context but does not uniquely locate a cell or site. ETSI TS 38.211, clause 7.4.2.1.

This distinction explains why 2,648,582 coverage PCI references do not mean that many towers. References repeat across grids, networks and channels.

The Downtown LA case: one identity, two estimates

The selected case uses NR 310/260, TAC 3960320, NCI 7587668269. The NCI’s hexadecimal form is 0x1C442A12D. The retained Apple row supplies NRARFCN 501390; the comparison provider did not return a channel.

The exact radio/MCC/MNC/TAC/NCI tuple associates both records with the same identity. Their displayed centers are 69.38 m apart. That is a reproducible estimate comparison, not independent confirmation of an antenna coordinate. The mirror attributes its data to OpenCellID; possible shared upstream provenance limits the sources’ independence. Its range describes coverage metadata, not an interchangeable accuracy radius.

The OpenCellID-derived cell data is licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0, with OpenCellID attribution requirements. This study selects public-area records, normalizes field labels and adds comparisons and context; the displayed estimate coordinates retain the source digits without rounding. This attribution does not clear the separate Apple-feed, mirror-service or City-data reuse questions.

The mirror’s unit/PCI field is null. Its first/last dates describe provider database history, not somebody’s movements. Nine reported samples are not nine independently verified observations.

At the Apple center, coverage grid C59904 contains same-network, same-channel PCIs 42 and 842. At the provider center, C59666 contains 42 and 866. Their shared 42 remains unassigned: it occurs in 76 retained grids, including 11 in the public study. The Apple uncertainty circle intersects 440 qualifying grids containing 145 distinct PCIs. There is no supported rule that assigns whichever PCI overlaps both estimate centers to this NCI.

Decimal places do not repair that gap. Neither proximity, a shared carrier nor an overlapping coverage area establishes a physical-site link.

What the parser resolved

Static examination of the retained native awd.metrics.CellularCoverageTile parser in the audited iOS 27.2.1 build 24B156 traced nine reachable message types and 34 declared fields. Reader, writer and independent converter/printer evidence established the important distinction:

  • LTE tag 5 is a repeated nested PCI message.
  • NR tag 6 is a repeated nested PCI message.
  • NR tag 5 is RedCapInfo, with configuration and count fields.

These human-readable labels come from native consumer evidence; the official .proto variable names were not recovered. The recognized coverage graph contains network, channel, band, counts, signal and PCI information, but no full ECI, full NCI, TAC or antenna-coordinate field. It therefore cannot supply the missing identity-to-site join. Unknown-field skipping also prevents treating this bounded schema as a claim about every possible future message.

Splitting NCI into a gNB prefix is another distinct problem. Standards allow a 22–32-bit gNB ID, so the target yields 11 possible arithmetic splits until the operator’s actual length is established. No split selects a PCI or coordinate; a logical gNB can also have distributed components. ETSI TS 38.413, clauses 9.3.1.6–7, ETSI TS 38.401.

What could establish the missing links?

Two evidence routes matter. A dated radio observation carrying PLMN, full NCI, TAC, PCI and channel together could support the identity-to-PCI relationship. Its GPS would describe the receiver’s position, not the antenna.

The open-source Network Survey format can represent those fields together; its pinned Android implementation and CSV writer preserve full NCI values and leave unavailable fields blank. This establishes a useful acquisition contract, not a measurement of the target. No matching export was obtained and no app was installed or survey started. Pinned messaging schema, Android record construction.

A dated authoritative crosswalk connecting the full identity to a specific installation could independently support the identity-to-site relationship, even without measured PCI. Municipal records offer a documentary route: current LA application instructions require antenna/cabinet coordinates and plans. Requirements alone do not identify this target, prove completion or establish current operation. LA City Planning CP13-7849, pages 5–6.

The reviewed evidence contains zero supported measured PCI-to-full-identity links and zero independently verified full-identity-to-installation links. Those are findings about this evidence set, not claims that sites or links do not exist.

Why this belongs on an ordinary computer

Location systems need databases that relate surrounding radio infrastructure to places. Apple describes using crowdsourced hotspot and cellular positions for positioning and describes supported iPhone contributions to that database. Those are published platform policies, not proof that this Mac contributed data or connected to these cells. Flyover resource locations likewise describe virtual map content, not visits. Apple Location Services & Privacy, Apple Maps & Privacy.

Location records deserve scrutiny: observations joined to time, a device or an account can reveal movement. This study does not establish that join, an unauthorized observer or remote control. No actual observation time was established for the mapped rows; acquisition and database dates must stay distinct from events.

The preview retains real public-test coordinates, identifiers, field names and limitations. It excludes private histories, Wi-Fi hardware addresses, credentials and personal machine/account metadata. The original evidence remains unredacted and unchanged. The interface preserves source attribution, exact technical values and coordinate-role explanations. No settings were changed and all serial devices were left closed. The authorized full-map research release retains the source-family qualifications in Data notices.
